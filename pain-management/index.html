The short answer

Pain management at our office starts with finding where your pain is coming from, then uses drug-free options such as chiropractic adjustments, soft-tissue work, corrective exercise and taping to ease it and help you move normally again.

Pain is a signal, and the most useful thing we can do first is work out what it is telling us. This page walks through your first consultation, the pain management options we use, how we choose between them, and when we send you to someone else instead.

What happens at your first pain consultation?

Your first visit is mostly listening and looking. We ask what you feel and where, when it started, how often it shows up, what makes it better or worse, and how it affects your work, sleep and family time. We also go over your health history and any medications, because pain rarely exists on its own.

Then we examine how you move. That usually includes posture, range of motion, joint movement, muscle tension, and simple strength and nerve checks. If something in your history or exam calls for imaging or a medical opinion first, we arrange or refer for it before starting care.

Before your visit, it helps to jot down a few notes:

  • When the pain started, and whether it followed an injury, a new job or a change in routine.
  • The times of day or positions when it is worst, and when it eases.
  • What you have already tried, and what helped even a little.
  • Any numbness, tingling or weakness, which tells us about nerve involvement.

Which drug-free options do we use for pain?

Most pain responds best to a combination of approaches rather than a single treatment. Depending on what we find, your plan may include:

  • Chiropractic adjustments, gentle and specific, to restore movement to joints that have become stiff and irritated.
  • Massage therapy to calm tight, guarded muscles and improve circulation around the sore area.
  • Corrective therapy, including assisted stretching, myofascial work and guided exercise, to rebuild strength and range of motion.
  • Kinesiology taping to support muscles and joints while you go about your day.
  • Home care advice on heat, ice, movement breaks and sleep positions.
  • Ergonomic and lifting guidance for work, whether that is a desk, a vehicle or a duty day.

Our care does not include narcotic pain medicine or injections. If we believe medication, injections or surgery should be considered, we will say so and point you toward the right medical provider.

How do we choose the right mix for you?

We match the plan to the source and stage of your pain. A fresh strain usually needs calming and gentle movement first. Pain that has built up over months often needs more strengthening and habit change. Nerve-related pain, like sciatica, calls for care that takes pressure off the nerve before pushing hard on stretching or strength.

Recent pain (days to weeks)

  • Calm the irritated area.
  • Restore gentle, pain-free movement.
  • Avoid the load that set it off while it settles.

Ongoing pain (months or more)

  • Rebuild strength and range of motion.
  • Change the daily habits that keep it coming back.
  • Plan ahead for flares so they pass faster.

When is pain a reason to see a doctor first?

Some symptoms need prompt medical attention rather than a chiropractic visit. Call your doctor or seek urgent care if pain comes with fever, unexplained weight loss, loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or inner thighs, sudden leg weakness, chest pain, or follows a serious fall or crash. If anything in your consultation raises a concern like this, we refer you before any care begins.

What does care cost, and do you take insurance?

No referral is needed to book. We take most health insurance plans, verify your coverage for free, and walk you through the numbers before anything begins.

  • No referral needed to book a first visit.
  • Free insurance verification before you come in.
  • Clear, up-front pricing if you are paying out of pocket.

We cannot promise a specific outcome or what your insurance will cover; every plan and every body is different. What we can promise is an honest look and a straight answer.

First-line

For low back pain, the American College of Physicians recommends that most patients start with non-drug treatments, which include options such as spinal manipulation, exercise, massage and heat.

Questions

Pain management questions we hear most

Do I need a referral for pain management?

No. You can book directly with our office. If you have a referral or records from another provider, bring them along; they help us see the full picture.

Will I need X-rays?

Not always. Many common pain problems can be assessed with a thorough history and exam. If your symptoms or exam suggest imaging would change the plan, we arrange or refer for it.

Can I keep taking my medication?

Yes. Keep following your doctor's advice about any medication. Our care works alongside it, and we never ask you to stop a prescription.

How soon should I feel a difference?

Many people notice some change within the first few visits, but it depends on how long the pain has been there and what is driving it. We give you a realistic window after your exam, not a guess.

Is pain management the same as management of pain?

They overlap. This page covers how we evaluate pain and the options we use. Our management of pain page focuses on long-term plans for pain that has lasted months or more.

The short answer

Most low back pain is mechanical, coming from the joints, discs and muscles of the lumbar spine. Chiropractic care uses gentle adjustments, soft-tissue work and guided movement to restore how that area moves, ease the pain, and lower the chance it comes back.

The low back, or lumbar spine, is the five vertebrae between your ribs and your pelvis. It bends, twists and carries load all day, which is exactly why it is so often the first place to complain. Here is what usually causes low back pain, when it needs a doctor, how chiropractic care helps, and what you can do today.

What causes low back pain?

Most low back pain comes from strain on structures that are healthy but overloaded, irritated or moving poorly. Less often, it comes from changes in a disc or joint that build over years. Common sources include:

  • Muscle and ligament strains from lifting, twisting or a sudden awkward movement.
  • Irritated facet joints, the small joints at the back of each vertebra that stiffen and lock up.
  • Disc problems, such as a bulging or herniated disc, which can also press on a nerve.
  • The sacroiliac joints, where the spine meets the pelvis, which can become painful and stiff.
  • Wear-related changes such as arthritis or degenerative disc changes, more common with age.
  • Long hours of sitting or standing, including desk work, driving and long duty days.

Your risk goes up with heavy or repetitive lifting, long periods without moving, carrying extra weight, a weak or deconditioned core, and previous back injuries. Many people in West Georgetown come in after a move, a long drive, a training day or a weekend project.

When should low back pain be checked by a doctor?

Most low back pain eases within days to a few weeks. Some symptoms need medical attention first. Call your doctor or go to urgent care if low back pain comes with:

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or inner thighs.
  • New or worsening weakness in a leg or foot.
  • Fever, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer.
  • Pain after a serious fall or car crash.
  • Pain that is severe at night and does not change with position.

If any of these come up during your first visit, we refer you to the right provider before starting care.

How does chiropractic care help low back pain?

Chiropractic care focuses on how your low back and pelvis move. Gentle, specific adjustments help restore motion to stiff joints, which can ease irritation and muscle guarding. Around that, your plan may include:

  • Spinal adjustments, with low-force options when your back is very sore.
  • Massage and soft-tissue work for tight muscles in the low back, hips and glutes.
  • Corrective therapy and guided exercise to build core and hip strength.
  • Kinesiology taping for support during work and daily activity.
  • Advice on sitting, lifting, sleeping and staying active while your back settles.

The first goal is to calm this episode. The second is to give you the strength and habits that keep your low back from becoming a regular problem.

What can you do for low back pain right now?

  1. Keep moving gentlyShort, easy walks and position changes usually help more than staying in bed, which tends to stiffen the back further.
  2. Use heat or iceMany people find heat eases muscle tension; others prefer a cold pack for a fresh strain. Use whichever feels better for 15 to 20 minutes at a time.
  3. Break up sittingStand up and move every 30 to 45 minutes, especially on long drives or desk days.
  4. Lift close and bend your kneesKeep loads close to your body and avoid twisting while lifting, at home and at work.
  5. Sleep in a supported positionTry a pillow between your knees on your side, or under your knees on your back.

First-line

For most people with low back pain, the American College of Physicians recommends starting with non-drug treatments, including options such as spinal manipulation, exercise, massage and heat.

Questions

Low back pain questions we hear most

Is low back pain the same as sciatica?

Not always. Low back pain stays mostly in the back or upper buttock. Sciatica is pain that travels along the sciatic nerve into the leg, often with tingling or numbness. They can happen together, and your exam helps us tell which is which.

Should I rest until it goes away?

A day of taking it easy can help, but long bed rest usually slows recovery. Gentle movement and normal activity, within comfort, tend to help the low back settle faster.

Do I need an MRI for low back pain?

Usually not to begin care. Most low back pain can be assessed with a careful history and exam. If your symptoms suggest a nerve or disc problem that imaging would clarify, we arrange or refer for it.

How many visits will I need?

It depends on how long the pain has been there and how your back responds. Many people notice the sharpest pain easing over the first couple of weeks. We give you a realistic plan after your first visit and adjust it as you improve.

Can chiropractic care help if my job is physical?

Yes, physical jobs are one of the most common reasons we see low back pain. Along with care, we give practical guidance on lifting, gear and recovery so your back can keep up with your work.
